The Kana Leech Hunt

Lesson 0006 · ~8 minutes · Reading track · Diagnostic — do this once

Five lessons of speaking, five perfect scores. Reading is the half that's been drifting: a year of on-and-off kana apps, and a handful still don't stick. This lesson finds out exactly which ones — and it's the only lesson in the course designed to make you score badly.

1. Why the app has been lying to you

There are two kinds of "knowing" a kana, and most apps only measure the useless one.

TypeWhat it feels likeSurvives a 3-week gap?
Fluency strengthYou just saw it, so it's freshNo
Storage strengthIt arrives instantly, cold, out of orderYes
The trap: multiple choice measures fluency. Picking ka from four options means eliminating three wrong answers — you can do that without recalling anything. Then the app marks the card "known," retires it, and you find out a year later that it never went in. Every question today is typed. No options to eliminate.

The second lie is speed. A kana you get right in four seconds isn't recalled, it's reconstructed — you walked the gojūon chant, or reasoned from a neighbour. That works at your desk and collapses in front of a train departure board. This drill times every answer. Under 1.8 seconds counts. Slower is logged as shaky and stays in rotation, no matter how right it was.

2. Your misses won't be random

You already gave me sixteen answers, and every single miss was a confusable pair — never a kana you'd simply never met:

ShownYou saidActuallyThe pair
オaoオ / ア
ろruroろ / る
クkekuク / ケ
ぬ—nuぬ / め

This is the whole problem in miniature. You don't have 15 unknown kana; you have a handful of pairs you've never been forced to tell apart. Shuffled review scatters る and ろ far from each other, so a coin-flip guess scores as knowledge. Fix the pairs and the "hard" kana mostly evaporate.

Which is why this drill does something no kana app does: miss one and it queues its twin a couple of cards later. You don't get to be lucky twice.

3. The diagnostic

All 92 — hiragana and katakana shuffled together, because gojūon order lets you chant your way to answers you don't actually have. It takes about eight minutes.

Three rules. No charts, no scrolling, no guessing-then-checking. Type what comes; if nothing comes in about three seconds, type anything and move on — a blank is data too. And it auto-advances, so don't hunt for a button.

One quirk: を must be typed wo, not o — otherwise there's no way to tell it apart from お.
