Trains and Tickets

Lesson 0012 · ~15 minutes · Speaking track · Builds on Lessons 2, 4, 5 and 11

The station is the highest-stakes place on the trip, and the one where the restaurant tricks stop working. You cannot point at a platform. You cannot see the fare. Nobody has time to watch you think.

So this lesson is short on new grammar and long on the two sentences that actually get you on the right train.

1. The only word you must have: まで

まで means as far as. Stick it after a place name and you have said "to there" — that is the whole rule, and it turns every station name into a sentence.

東京まで Toukyou made to Tokyo / as far as Tokyo

Now combine it with two frames you already own:

東京まで いくらですか Toukyou made ikura desu ka How much to Tokyo?
東京まで、おとな ひとり おねがいします Toukyou made, otona hitori onegaishimasu One adult to Tokyo, please.
おとな is adult, and ひとり / ふたり are the people-counters from Lesson 11 — still not ひとつ. At a ticket window that one sentence does the entire transaction.

Buy one at any machine, load it with cash, and you stop buying tickets entirely. The word for topping it up is the English one: チャージ — chaaji.

3. Which platform

Platforms are numbered, and the counter is ～ばんせん:

何番線ですか nanbansen desu ka Which platform?

The answer is a number you already have from Lesson 2, plus ばんせん:

You will not catch the number by ear at first. That is fine and expected. Say すみません and hold up your phone — every station has the platform written in numerals on a sign, and staff will point at it. Lesson 5's skill, doing real work.

4. The sentence that saves the trip

One new pattern, and it is the most valuable thing in this lesson. You are on a platform, a train is sitting there, and you need to know if it is yours.

この電車は 京都に 行きますか kono densha wa Kyouto ni ikimasu ka Does this train go to Kyoto?

Break it down — three of the four pieces are already yours:

このthis (Lesson 5's ko-so-a-do ladder)
電車train — densha
～にto ～ (destination)
行きますかdoes it go? — ikimasu ka
Ask it even when you are fairly sure. It is a yes/no question, so the answer is one of two words you already know — はい or いいえ — plus, if it is いいえ, a pointed finger at the right platform. This is the rare Japanese question where you are guaranteed to understand the reply. Use it constantly.

6. When it goes wrong

One word, because it will happen:

のりかえ norikae transfer / change trains

If someone says it to you while pointing, you are being told to get off and change. Say どこで のりかえですか — where do I change? — and follow the finger.

どこで のりかえですか doko de norikae desu ka Where do I change?

8. Check yourself

9. Real-world rep (this week)

Two things, both small.

Say the platform sentence at your own transit stop. Out loud, with a real destination: この電車は ___ に 行きますか. Swap the place name each time. It is the one sentence here you will use daily and it needs to come out without assembling it.

Read every katakana sign you pass. Not Japanese ones — any of them. Menus, packaging, brand names. Lesson 10 proved that reading real words in context moves your kana faster than the drill does, and タ and ク are the two you most need the reps on.

10. What's next

Hotels — checking in, asking about breakfast, and getting your bags held after checkout. Lower stakes than the station, and it is the last of the four places you cannot avoid.
