The Two Questions That Unlock Japan

Lesson 0004 · ~14 minutes · Speaking-first · Builds on: kudasai, ikura desu ka, greetings

You can already greet, order, and ask prices. Today: asking whether a shop has something, and asking where anything is — the toilet, the station, the ATM. With these two questions you can navigate any shop, station, or street in the country.

New this lesson: you've now read kana three lessons straight without missing — so words you already know appear in kana only. すみません, ください, これ — you own these. Romaji stays for new words only. If something is hard to read, that's the rep working; hit 🔊 and sound it out.

1. ___ wa arimasu ka? — "Do you have ___?"

＿＿ は ありますか？ ___ wa arimasu ka? "Do you have ___?" / "Is there ___?"

Arimasu means "exists / there is." Add ka — the spoken question mark — and it flips into a question. The particle wa you already know from こんにちは and kore wa ikura desu ka: it marks what you're asking about. So literally: as-for coffee — exists?

You want to know if they have…AskHear it
wifiワイファイは ありますか
an English menu (eigo no menyuu)えいごの メニューは ありますか
a bigger size (ookii no — a big one)おおきいのは ありますか

The answer comes back as arimasu (we have it) or arimasen / nai desu (we don't). Then you already know the next move: これを ください.

2. ___ wa doko desu ka? — "Where is ___?"

＿＿ は どこですか？ ___ wa doko desu ka? "Where is ___?"

Same skeleton — thing + wa + question + ka — just swap arimasu for doko desu ("where is"). Feel how the pattern repeats: you're not learning sentences anymore, you're learning one machine with swappable parts.

You don't need to understand the answer. Directions come back fast and complicated — everywhere on Earth. Watch the pointing, catch asoko (over there) or massugu (straight ahead), bow slightly, ありがとうございます, and follow the finger. Asking the question is 90% of the job.

3. Put it together — the shop run

Full sequence, all four lessons working at once. Say each line out loud:

こんにちは。
すみません、えいごの メニューは ありますか？
じゃあ、これを ください。ビールも ふたつ ください。
ありがとうございます！ Konnichiwa. → Sumimasen, eigo no menyuu wa arimasu ka? → Jaa, kore o kudasai. Biiru mo futatsu kudasai. → Arigatou gozaimasu! Hello → excuse me, English menu? → then this one please, and two beers too → thanks!

Two tiny new pieces hiding in there: jaa ("well then…") and mo ("also" — it replaces o when you mean "and X too"). Don't drill them; just notice them.

5. Real-world rep (this week)

Play "doko desu ka" in your own house: keys — kagi wa doko desu ka? Phone — sumaho wa doko desu ka? Ask it out loud every time you look for something. The pattern will be automatic before you ever board the plane.
