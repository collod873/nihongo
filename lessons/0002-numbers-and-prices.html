Two Beers and the Bill, Please

Lesson 0002 · ~13 minutes · Speaking-first · Builds on: sumimasen + ___ o kudasai

Last time you learned to ask for one of anything. Today you get numbers — so you can order two beers, understand what things cost, and ask the single most useful shopping question in Japan: how much is this?

1. Numbers 1–10 — the whole foundation

Japanese numbers are wonderfully regular: learn 1–10 and you can build almost everything else (11 is "ten-one," 20 is "two-ten"). Say each one out loud as you go — even beats, like sumimasen.

Memory hooks: ichi-ni-san you may already know from karate or anime countdowns. Go = 5 (golf: "give me a 5-iron, go!"). Roku = 6 — a rock has 6 sides if it's a cube. Silly hooks work; use whatever sticks.

2. Prices — hundreds, thousands, yen

Three more words and every price tag in Japan unlocks:

WordJapaneseMeaning
hyaku ひゃく100
sen せん1,000
en えんyen (¥)

Stack them like Lego: go-hyaku en = 500 yen. Sen en = 1,000 yen. Ni-sen go-hyaku en = 2,500 yen. A vending-machine coffee is about hyaku-go-juu en (150). Don't drill big numbers yet — in real life the register shows the total. What matters is recognizing the pattern when the cashier says it.

いくらですか？ Ikura desu ka? "How much is it?"
Combo with last lesson: point at anything → Sumimasen, kore wa ikura desu ka? — "Excuse me, how much is this?" (The little wa just marks what you're asking about — don't sweat it yet.)

3. Ordering two of something

Here's a Japanese quirk: when ordering, you usually don't use ichi, ni, san directly — you use general counting words. You only need three:

How manyJapaneseSay it
oneひとつhitotsu
twoふたつfutatsu
threeみっつmittsu

The pattern: [thing] o [count] kudasai — the count slides in right before kudasai:

ビールを ふたつ ください。 Biiru o futatsu kudasai. "Two beers, please."

Say it out loud five times. Then swap pieces: koohii o hitotsu kudasai, mizu o futatsu kudasai. More than three of something? Just point and hold up fingers — completely normal in Japan, and kore o kudasai plus fingers always works.

4. Check yourself

5. Real-world rep (this week)

Count real things out loud in Japanese: stairs as you climb them, reps at the gym, items going into a grocery bag. Ichi, ni, san… — numbers only become automatic through dumb repetition, and counting real objects beats flashcards.
