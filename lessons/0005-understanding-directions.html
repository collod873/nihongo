When They Answer, Understand Them

Lesson 0005 · ~14 minutes · Listening-first · Builds on: どこですか

Last lesson you learned to ask どこですか. Today the answers stop being noise. Six words cover nearly every direction a stranger will give you — and for the first time, this lesson tests your ears: some questions play audio with no text. That's what Japan actually does to you.

1. Here, there, over there

Japanese splits "there" by distance — a three-step ladder away from you:

WordKanaMeaningHear it
kokoここhere (by me)
sokoそこthere (by you / near)
asokoあそこover there (away from both)
Spot the family: こ-, そ-, あ- … you already know これ (this one). Same ladder: これ this / それ that / あれ that over there. Japanese reuses this ko-so-a pattern everywhere — learn the ladder once, get whole word families free.

The answer you'll hear most when you ask where something is: あそこです — "it's over there" — plus a pointing hand.

2. Straight, right, left

WordKanaMeaningHear it
massuguまっすぐstraight ahead
migiみぎright
hidariひだりleft
Memory hooks: massugu — "must go" straight. migi = right: you write with your mighty right (if you do). hidari = left: what's left when the mighty hand is busy. Silly sticks.

Real answers glue these together with bits you can ignore. まっすぐいって、みぎです — "go straight, then it's on the right." You only need to catch massugu … migi. The rest is packaging.

The traveler's filter: when directions come at full speed, listen ONLY for the six words of this lesson plus pointing. Nobody catches everything — natives don't need to be fully understood, just followed. Two keywords and a finger beat a perfect translation.

5. Real-world rep (this week)

Narrate your driving or walking, out loud: every turn is migi or hidari, every straightaway massugu. A week of narrated errands wires these deeper than any flashcard deck — and it's hands-free.
