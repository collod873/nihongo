The ne Hub

Lesson 0009 · ~15 minutes · Reading track · Both scripts

Lesson 7 worked. You ran the hook family twice: 22/27, then 26/27 two minutes later. ヌ and ラ went from misses to mastered inside one sitting. That's the third time concentrated contrast has fixed something for you, and it's now the format.

This lesson does the same thing to your other hub — the one where four different characters all came back ne.

1. Unfinished business: ワ

One character survived Lesson 7. You missed ワ in both runs, and the second time you typed fu — fast, at 1.8 seconds. It's the only leech in your whole record.

Why ワ and not the others. Every other hook character differs from フ by something added: ス kicks out a leg, ヌ crosses it, ヲ lays a bar across the top. You can look for those. ワ adds nothing — it just closes. So here's the check that works, and it's the only one you need:

How many ends of the top bar come down?
フ — one end. The left stays open.
ワ — both ends. It's a box with the bottom missing.

Nothing else in this lesson touches ワ. Ask that one question next time it appears and it's done.

2. The hub: four characters, one wrong answer

From your full 92, counting what you typed:

You typedTimesWhen you actually saw
ne4チ · ナ (katakana) · れ · ぬ (hiragana)

This one is stranger than the fu hub. Fu swallowed four characters that genuinely look alike. Ne swallows characters from both scripts at once — and チ looks nothing like ぬ.

So this isn't a shape problem. It's a sound problem: ne is what your mouth reaches for when a character feels familiar but won't resolve. The fix is the same either way — give each character a feature you check before the guess fires — but you'll be doing it twice, once per script.

3. Katakana: everything is ノ plus one thing

Same trick as the hook family. One base shape, one addition at a time.

KanaSound= ノ plus…
ノnonothing. One diagonal. This is the base.
メmea stroke crossing the other way — an X
ナnaa flat bar across — a plus sign
チchiナ + a short tick above the bar
ネneチ + a foot at the bottom left
One question, in order:
Crossed like an X? → メ
Crossed like a +? → then look up. Nothing above → ナ. A tick → チ. A tick and a foot below → ネ.
Not crossed at all? → ノ

ネ is the most decorated character in the family — a tick on top, a bar, a stem, and a foot. That's the useful thing about it. If what you're looking at is plain, it is not ne. Your four ne answers were all given to characters simpler than ネ.

4. Hiragana: does it have a stem?

The hiragana side is easier than it looks, because three of the four are the same character with a different tail. They share an identical left stem — a short vertical with a kink.

KanaSoundStem?How the right side ends
わwayessweeps down and left, open — nothing at the end
れreyesflicks up and out to the right
ねneyescurls into a loop
ぬnunoone continuous curve, loop at the end
Check the left side first. A straight vertical stem → it's わ, れ or ね. No stem, all curves → it's ぬ (or め).

Then the tail: open → わ · up-flick → れ · loop → ね.

That first check alone kills half the hub. You read ぬ as ne — but ぬ has no stem at all, and ね always does. The two are only similar in that both end in a loop, and the loop is the last thing to look at, not the first.

A free one. わ is wa — the same sound as ワ, your one remaining leech. Different shapes, one sound, no rule to learn. Two of the nine characters in today's drill are answers you already half-know.

5. Drill it now, in one sitting

Nine characters, 27 cards, both scripts interleaved. Miss one and you get the discriminating feature plus the character you confused it with, a couple of cards later. Do not warm up and do not stop halfway — the concentration is the active ingredient.

Under 1.8 seconds counts as solid. Slower than that is correct-but-reconstructed, and it stays in rotation. Last time your family drill came back 67% solid — that's the number to beat, not the score.
