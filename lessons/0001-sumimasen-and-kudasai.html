The Two Words That Run Japan

Lesson 0001 · ~12 minutes · Speaking-first · Mission: real conversations while traveling

With exactly two building blocks — sumimasen and ___ o kudasai — you can get a waiter's attention, order anything on a menu, buy anything in a shop, and apologize for bumping into someone on the train. This is the single highest-value pattern in travel Japanese, and it's your first win.

1. Sumimasen — the Swiss Army word

すみません sumimasen "Excuse me" / "Sorry" / "Thanks for the trouble"

One word, three jobs: getting attention (excuse me, waiter!), light apology (sorry, my bad), and thanks-with-apology (someone holds a door). When in doubt in Japan, sumimasen is almost never wrong. Tofugu has a full breakdown of why this one word does so much work.

Pronunciation rule #1: Japanese syllables get even weight — su-mi-ma-se-n, five even beats, no stressed syllable like English "ex-CUSE me." Say it flat and steady. This one habit will do more for being understood than any vocabulary.

2. ___ o kudasai — the universal request

＿＿ を ください ___ o kudasai "___, please." (literally: please give me ___)

Drop any noun in the blank and you have a complete, polite sentence. The little word o (written を) is a grammar marker that points at the thing you want — think of it as an arrow: water ← please-give. You don't need to understand particles deeply yet; just keep o glued in front of kudasai.

The traveler's cheat code: これをください — kore o kudasai — "this one, please." Point at anything (menu photo, item on a shelf, plastic food model in the window) and say it. You can now order anything in Japan without knowing its name.

3. The full exchange

Here's the whole restaurant move, start to finish:

すみません！ これをください。 Sumimasen! Kore o kudasai. "Excuse me! This one, please."

Now say it out loud five times — actually out loud, even beats, no stress accent. Use the 🔊 button, echo it, repeat. Speaking-first means your mouth practices, not just your eyes.
