Lesson 0008 · ~15 minutes · Speaking track · Review — answer before you read

You scored 3/3, 5/5, 5/5 and 5/5 on Lessons 1–4. That was six weeks ago, and you've told me some of it has gone soft. Both things are true, and this lesson is how we find out which parts.

1. Why four perfect scores prove less than you'd think

You met the material minutes before each quiz. That measures fluency strength — how easily something comes when it's already warm. It's real, but it fades.

What matters for a trip is storage strength: does it arrive cold, six weeks later, out of order, when a waiter is looking at you. Nothing has ever tested that. Six weeks of silence is, awkwardly, the perfect experiment — and it's already been run. This just reads the result.

So answer first, read second. The refresher is below the quiz, deliberately. Scrolling down before you answer converts a real measurement into another fluency test, and we already have four of those. A blank is fine — a blank is data.

2. The cold check

Ten questions, shuffled across all five lessons rather than grouped. That's on purpose too: grouped questions let you stay in one lesson's mindset, which is not how a conversation works. Mixed is harder and it's the thing that actually sticks.

3. The refresher

Now read. Anything you missed above is worth saying aloud three times — that's the rep that moves it from recognised to available.

The four sentence frames

Nearly every transaction on your trip is one of these four with a different noun dropped in.

FrameMeansUse it when
___ をください
___ o kudasai		___, pleaseYou can see it or point at it
___ はありますか
___ wa arimasu ka		Do you have ___?You are not sure they have it
___ はどこですか
___ wa doko desu ka		Where is ___?You need to find something
いくらですか
Ikura desu ka		How much is it?Any time money is involved
The one that unlocks the rest: すみません sumimasen — excuse me, sorry, and thanks for the trouble, all in one. Put it in front of any frame above and you are being polite. It is the single highest-value word you know.

Numbers: ひゃく hyaku = 100, せん sen = 1000, えん en = yen. Counting things: ひとつ one, ふたつ two, みっつ three — these go after the noun: ビールをふたつください.

4. The gap: Lesson 5's directions

Lesson 5 went out six weeks ago and you never finished it — the three audio-only questions needed a Japanese voice on your phone, and if that voice was missing the lesson simply stopped. That was a design mistake on my part. Nothing here depends on audio. The 🔊 buttons are a bonus; if they are silent, you have lost nothing.

Six words carry almost every direction you will be given:

WordKanaMeaningHear it
kokoここhere, by me
sokoそこthere, by you
asokoあそこover there, away from both
massuguまっすぐstraight ahead
migiみぎright
hidariひだりleft
The traveller's filter. When directions arrive at full speed, listen only for these six words plus the pointing hand. まっすぐいって、みぎです is just massugu … migi with packaging around it. Natives do not need to be fully understood, only followed. Two keywords and a finger beat a perfect translation.

5. Real-world rep

Next time you buy anything at all, run the whole chain out loud, even to yourself: Sumimasen → kore o kudasai → ikura desu ka → arigatou gozaimasu. Four beats, one transaction. That is the entire speaking track working as a unit, and saying it once in the world is worth more than re-reading this page twice.
