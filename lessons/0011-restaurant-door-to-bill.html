The Restaurant, Door to Bill

Lesson 0011 · ~15 minutes · Speaking track · Builds on Lessons 1, 2 and 4

Back to talking. One continuous scene — walking in, being seated, ordering, asking what's good, paying, leaving — and almost all of it is assembly from things you already have.

But first, the one thing your last two lessons say you actually need.

1. The four frames — your real gap

In Lesson 8 you were asked how to check whether a pharmacy stocks band-aids. In Lesson 10 you were asked how to order an iced coffee off a menu. Both questions offered the same four options. You missed both.

FrameMeans
～を くださいgive me ～
～は ありますかdo you have ～?
～は いくらですかhow much is ～?
～は どこですかwhere is ～?

You know all four — you've produced each of them correctly in an earlier lesson. That's exactly what makes this the same problem as your kana: the pieces are all there, and the mapping is what slips. When four options are all familiar and all grammatical, the wrong one comes out fast and confident.

One question fixes it. Ask yourself: what don't I know?
Nothing — I've decidedを ください
Whether they have itは ありますか
The priceは いくらですか
Where it isは どこですか
The frame answers the gap. If there's no gap — the thing is printed on the menu in front of you — you're not asking, you're ordering.

Section 6 drills exactly this, five times in a row. That's deliberate: massed contrast is what cleared your kana, and this is the same shape of problem.

2. Walking in

The moment you open the door, the whole staff shouts:

いらっしゃいませ irasshaimase Welcome! (said to customers)
You do not reply to this. It's a shop noise, not a greeting — nobody expects an answer, and answering marks you out more than saying nothing. Nod, or don't. That's it. You'll hear it in every shop and konbini in the country.

Then they'll ask how many of you there are:

何名様ですか nanmei sama desu ka How many people?

Answer with a number of people:

PeopleSayHear it
oneひとりです
twoふたりです
threeさんにんです
Careful — these are not the counters from Lesson 2. ひとつ / ふたつ count things. ひとり / ふたり count people. Same idea, different word, and mixing them up is the single most common beginner slip in this scene. Holding up fingers also works and nobody minds.

3. Getting the menu, and ordering

You already read メニュー last lesson. Now ask for one — and it's the ください frame, because there's nothing you don't know:

メニューを ください menyuu o kudasai The menu, please.

To order, the same frame plus a count from Lesson 2:

ビールを ふたつ ください biiru o futatsu kudasai Two beers, please.

And when you can't name it, Lesson 4's move still works — point and say これを ください. Pointing at a menu photo is not cheating; it is what the photos are for.

4. Asking what's good

One new phrase, and it's the highest-value sentence in the lesson:

おすすめは 何ですか osusume wa nan desu ka What do you recommend?
Why this one is worth more than it looks. You will not understand the answer — it'll come back fast and full of food words you don't have. Doesn't matter. Ask it, watch where they point, and say じゃあ、それを ください (jaa, sore o kudasai — then that one, please). You've just had a real exchange and ordered something a local chose.

5. ください or おねがいします?

A fifth frame, and it overlaps the first — so here's the honest version rather than a rule that breaks.

を くださいおねがいします
Best forthings you can name or point atservices, requests, and the bill
Tonedirect, perfectly politea touch softer
Ordering food✓✓ — both are fine

For ordering, use whichever comes out. For anything that isn't an object, use おねがいします — which is exactly what you need next.

6. The bill, and leaving

おかいけい おねがいします okaikei onegaishimasu The bill, please.

Catch a server's eye with すみません first — Lesson 1's word, still doing the heavy lifting. In many places you'll be waved toward a till by the door rather than handed a bill at the table; follow the pointing, which is Lesson 5's skill.

On the way out, the one phrase that will genuinely delight people:

ごちそうさまでした gochisousama deshita Thank you for the meal.
Said to whoever fed you, on the way out, every time. It's long — break it into four beats: go-chi-sou-sama + deshita. Nobody will mind if you fumble it; they will notice that you tried.

Nine turns, door to street. Six of them you already had before today.

8. Check yourself

The first five all offer the same four frames. That's the point — pick by asking what you don't know.

9. Real-world rep (this week)

Run the scene out loud, alone, start to finish — all nine turns, playing both parts. It takes ninety seconds. Do it before you eat anything this week and the sequence will be automatic long before you need it.

Then the frame drill, wherever you are: every time you want something today, ask yourself which of the four you'd use. Coffee you can see and have decided on? ください. Not sure the shop stocks it? ありますか. That's free reps and it costs no time.

10. What's next

Trains and tickets — buying, finding the platform, and confirming you're on the right one. Highest-stakes situation on the trip, and the one where the four frames earn their keep.

Kana lives in the trainer. Your leeches moved: it's hiragana now, and ま is the worst of them by a distance.
