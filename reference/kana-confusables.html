Confusable Kana

Reference · The ~20 pairs that account for nearly all kana errors

Almost nobody fails at kana by meeting an unfamiliar character. They fail at pairs — two shapes that differ by one loop, one tick, or one stroke direction. Shuffled review scatters the twins so a coin-flip scores as knowledge. This sheet is the discrimination rule for each pair. Learn the rule once and the pair stops costing you anything.

1. The big one: シ ツ ソ ン

Four characters, one family, responsible for more katakana errors than everything else combined. Shape won't save you — the fonts differ too much. Stroke direction will, and it resolves on two independent questions.

Marks enter from the left, sweeping up →Marks drop from the top, sweeping down ↓
Two marks シ shi ツ tsu
One mark ン n ソ so
How to read it in the wild: count the marks first — two means shi/tsu, one means n/so. Then look at where the marks sit: hugging the left edge and angled like a smile means shi or n; sitting along the top and angled like a frown means tsu or so.

Mnemonic that survives panic: shi and n both come in low and left. Shin — the low, left one. The other two are the top pair.

Their hiragana counterparts are no help — し (shi), つ (tsu), そ (so), ん (n) look nothing like these. Treat the katakana four as their own problem.

2. The hat rule

Two pairs where the only difference is a small stroke on top. Same rule, both scripts:

BareWearing the hatRule
つ tsuう uThe hat makes it a vowel.
ワ waウ uSame again — hat means u.

3. Loop or no loop

PairHas the loopDoesn'tRule
る / ろる ruろ roru curls closed at the bottom; ro ends open.
ぬ / めぬ nuめ menu has the loop — like a noodle. me is bare.
ね / わ / れね neわ wa · れ reClosed loop = ne. Round open curve = wa. Straight flick = re.

4. Count the crossbars

OneTwoRule
さ saき kiJust count the bars: 1 = sa, 2 = ki. Same stem underneath.
は haほ ho1 = ha, 2 = ho. Same left stem on both.

ち (chi) is さ (sa) mirrored — the curve hangs right instead of left. And ま (ma) looks like は (ha) but its strokes cross a single vertical, where は has a separate stem on the left.

5. Cross or meet

Strokes crossStrokes only meetRule
ヌ nuス suCrossing makes it nu.
メ meナ name is an X, na is a plus.
チ chiテ techi's top stroke slants across; te's is a flat separate line.
ケ keク kuke has a stroke cutting the diagonal; ku has only the top-left tick.

6. Katakana odds and ends

PairRule
ホ ho / オ oho has two loose side dots; o is connected through.
ラ ra / フ fura has a separate short stroke on top; fu is one plain bend.
マ ma / ム muma's corner is top-right; mu's is bottom-left.
コ ko / ユ yu / ロ roko is an open bracket; yu has a long bottom bar; ro is a closed box.
ア a / マ maa's inner stroke hangs down from the crossbar; ma's sweeps from the corner.

7. Hiragana odds and ends

PairRule
い i / り rii is two short separate marks; ri is taller with a curve.
こ ko / い iko is two horizontal strokes; i is two vertical ones.
す su / お oo has the extra dot on the right; su doesn't.
せ se / ち chise has a crossbar through the vertical; chi has none.

8. The near-identical twins

A few characters are genuinely almost the same in both scripts. There is no shape rule — context decides, and context is reliable, because katakana clusters in foreign words and hiragana in grammar:

へ ヘ · り リ · か カ · や ヤ · も モ · せ セ · ら ラ

へ and ヘ (both he) are the worst of these — and mercifully they're the same sound, so misreading the script costs you nothing.

The payoff: there are 92 basic kana, but only about 20 real decisions. Everything on this sheet is a rule you apply once and then stop paying for. Drill them in the Kana Trainer on Confusables only.
